Proposal A, expanded

Emberward

Carry the last lantern down into a drowned city. Your deck walks behind you in a line, and every ember you burn to reach past the front is one you can't spend at the market.

  • Paid reach
  • Three-copy Rekindling
  • 60-card pool
  • Deep drafting
  • Desktop and Steam

Feels like Slay the Spire's run, Gatebreaker's ordered deck, TFT's triples and economy, a Magic draft's signposts.

Rules

Battle, updated

The board
Unchanged. Two 4×3 grids face each other across the Gap, and lanes A to D are shared. Rows resolve Front, then Mid, then Back.
Waves
Up to six. Each wave, you and the enemy alternate single actions, two each (three in wave 1), then the Clash resolves.
Your Procession
Your deck is laid out face-up in order. The first two cards still in it are lit. See the next section.
Play
Take one card from your Procession and either Summon it as a Kindled (free) or Cast its spell (pay its ember cost; it resolves at once). Lit cards cost nothing extra. Reaching deeper costs 1✦ for each place the card sits behind the lit two: the 3rd card costs 1✦, the 5th costs 3✦.
Pass
End your part of the wave. The first side to pass each wave gains 1✦.
Move
Not an action. Swift steps are free each wave; extra steps cost 1✦ each; Rooted units never move.
The enemy's Procession
Enemies have a Procession too, shown face-up above their grid. Their two lit cards are their telegraph: you know what is coming next, and you can see when they pay to reach for something nastier.
Winning
Bring the enemy Warden to 0. Open lanes let Strike, Shoot and Pierce hit the Warden. After wave 6, the side that dealt more face damage wins.

Gatebreaker’s paid reach, adapted

The Procession: play out of order, for a price

Your deck is no longer a pile you pick from freely. It is a Procession: a single line of face-up cards in the order you set. The first two cards still in the line are lit and free to play. Anything further back can be played right now too, but the lantern has to reach for it, and that costs 1✦ for every place the card sits behind the light: 1✦ for the 3rd card, 2✦ for the 4th, and so on. Skipped cards stay where they are and become lit as the cards ahead of them leave.

Why it matters
Embers are already the shop money and the spell money. Now they are also how you change your mind mid-battle. A well-ordered Procession plays itself for free; a bad one makes you pay for every correction, and that ember is gone from your interest and your next market.
Setting the order
New cards join the back. Before each battle, the Muster screen shows the enemy's first wave and their Procession, and you may make two free moves (lift a card and put it anywhere). Each further move costs 1✦. The order then stays fixed for the battle and carries into the next one.
What a reach buys
Answers. Their lit cards say a Cleave unit is arriving in lane C, so you reach for the Bell Warden with Taunt; their Warden is at 6 and lane A is open, so you reach for the Marrowhound. Paid reach is the release valve that keeps a face-up, no-draw deck from feeling scripted.
Tools that bend the line
Candlewright's Light the Way makes your next reach free. Gravedigger's Dig Up moves any card to the front. The Ferryman Warden's first reach each battle is free. Ferryman's Pole and Long Wick are relics built around it. Reach is a resource that cards and relics can trade in.
The enemy plays by it too
Enemy Processions are hand-built per encounter. Elites carry one or two cards they will pay to reach for when you open a lane, so watching their embers tells you when that's likely.
Tuning knobs
Lit window (2), reach price (1✦ per card), free Muster moves (2). If playtests find the Muster fiddly, make it free and let in-battle reach carry the tension alone.

Your three-copy merge, made central

Rekindling: three copies, one Bright card

The moment you own three copies of a card, they Rekindle: the three flames braid into a single Bright card. It's offered automatically when the third copy arrives, from a salvage pick, the market or an event. You can postpone it to the next Hearth or market if you want the copies for now.

×2.5 per actionThree Squires take three actions and three cells to deliver 6 Power. One Bright Squire delivers 5 Power plus Cleave in a single action and cell. Actions and cells are what a wave runs short of.

The rule of thumb
Bright = roughly double stats, the keyword stepped up a tier, and a spell that is stronger and 1✦ cheaper. Each Bright form is hand-written, so the merge feels like a payoff rather than a number change.
Where it sits in the line
The Bright card takes the slot of the front-most copy. Owning three copies of an early card is the cheapest way to make your opening strong.
The trade-off
Three bodies cover three lanes and count three times for clusters. One Bright card covers one lane. Hollow decks that want bodies sometimes postpone; Bell and Lamp decks almost always merge.
Rares
Rares can Rekindle too, but you'd need a boss pick and two Echoes to get there. Their Bright forms break a rule, as boss rewards should.
What it replaced
Mid-battle twin fusion and the 16-card cap are gone. One merge rule, applied in the draft, does both jobs and gives drafting its main long-term goal.

Echoes: help finishing triples

An Echo is a copy of a card you already own, marked so you can spot it. Echoes favour cards you own two of, so a triple is usually one good salvage away. The Echo Shell relic guarantees one in every salvage. This is the system that makes Rekindling a plan rather than luck.

Omens: reading the draft

At the start of each act, two Orders wax. Their cards, and the bridge card between them, show up 1.5× as often in salvage and at the market for the whole act. The map shows the Omens before your first pick, the way a Magic drafter reads what's flowing. Going with the Omens makes triples easier; going against them gets you cards no one else would.

How big should the Procession be?

First fight
9 cards: a 6-card starter and 3 opening picks.
End of Act I
12 to 14 cards, often with one Bright card.
Act III
14 to 18 cards with 2 to 4 Bright cards. Battles run 11 to 13 plays, so cards past about 14th place rarely see play without a reach.
The pressure
There is no cap. Bloat costs you naturally, because every card you add pushes something else further from the light. Skipping a salvage for 3✦ is often correct by Act II.

Six archetypes, one for each pair

Each pair has a bridge uncommon that signposts it, like the gold cards in a Magic set. Seeing one in an early pack is a strong hint to move in.

Lamp + Bell

Lighthouse

A Bell wall holds the Front row while Lamp artillery burns from behind. The safest first draft: Bell keeps you alive while Lamp deals the damage.

Signpost: Beacon Keeper
Look for: Tallow Bulwark, Tower Archer, Kiln Mortar, Wickmonger, Great Bell

Lamp + Hollow

Pyre

Hollow bodies die on purpose and their Last Gasps set the enemy alight. Aggressive and explosive, weak to enemies that don't need to attack.

Signpost: Pyre Wight
Look for: Bone Gnawer, Ember Hound, Gloomshot, Crypt Keeper, Lamplighter's Ghost

Lamp + Brine

Scald

Stack Burn and Poison on the same targets and let them tick. Slow to start, impossible to out-heal by wave 4.

Signpost: Scalding Font
Look for: Tallow Imp, Drowned Archer, Rot Priest, Stoke (Brazier Golem), Mother of Silt

Hollow + Bell

Ossuary

A wall that refuses to stay dead. Taunt units return and Wisps refill the gaps, so you win the long game on face damage.

Signpost: Bone Bellwether
Look for: Gargoyle, Ossuary Knight, Chain Acolyte, Wisp Herder, The Bronze Abbess

Hollow + Brine

Drowned

Poison weakens the enemy and every poisoned death becomes a Wisp on your side. The board slowly tips your way.

Signpost: Drowned Bride
Look for: Barnacle Thrall, Gloomshot, Pearl Diver, Choir of Bones, Kraken of the Undercroft

Brine + Bell

Undertow

Pull enemies into lanes held by poisonous Taunt units. A control deck that wins by attrition and spends embers on pulls.

Signpost: Barnacle Abbot
Look for: Moray, Siren of Vael, Tallow Bulwark, Iron Penitent, Leviathan Calf

Wardens and starting Processions

Each Warden starts with six cards and a relic. Every starter holds at least one pair, so a single matching pick in the opening draft sets up an early Rekindle, and your first pack always comes from your Warden's Order.

Try the draft. Pick a Warden, take the three opening packs, then play through an Act I path of salvage picks, two markets, an elite and the boss reward. The odds, Omens, Echoes and Rekindling all follow the rules above. Battles aren't simulated; each one assumes you spend a few embers on reach and spells (3✦ a fight, 5✦ an elite, 6✦ the boss) before it pays out.

      A draft, played out

      One Act I run with the Ferryman, pick by pick, showing the reasoning a good player would use.

      1. Start

        The Ferryman. Starter: Drowned Archer ×2, Moray ×2, Pearl Diver, Ferry Boatman. Omens this act: Brine and Bell wax, so the Undertow bridge (Barnacle Abbot) will turn up more often.

      2. Pack 1
        Drowned ArcherKelp WraithSiren of Vael

        Siren is the stronger card, but a third Drowned Archer Rekindles straight away into a 4/4 that applies Poison 3, and the Procession drops from 7 cards to 5 before the first fight.

      3. Pack 2
        Tallow BulwarkEmber HoundGloomshot

        A Taunt wall for the Front row. Bell is waxing, and Bulwark plus poison is the Undertow plan.

      4. Pack 3
        Barnacle AbbotWickmongerMoray

        The signpost. The third Moray was tempting, but the Abbot confirms the lane: Brine + Bell, pull enemies into poisonous Taunt.

      5. Fight 1
        Siren of VaelBat SwarmWandering Squire

        The Siren comes back around, and it's the pull engine the deck wanted. Muster: move it to 2nd place with a free move.

      6. Fight 2
        MorayIron PenitentCandlewright

        An Echo of Moray. A third copy Rekindles, and the line is back to 7 cards with two Bright ones.

      7. Market
        Barnacle ThrallGreat BellKraken of the Undercroft

        21✦ banked. The Kraken (8✦) is a bomb, but buying it drops the next interest payment from +4 to +2. Took Thrall (3✦), kept +3 interest, and held the Kraken's slot for next visit.

      8. Elite
        Iron PenitentBrazier GolemOssuary Knight

        A second Taunt in the Front row. Relic: Ferryman's Pole, so reaching up to two past the lit cards is free, which suits a deck that pays for answers.

      9. Boss
        Leviathan CalfThe Sun FurnaceThe Lich of Vael

        Leviathan Calf with Persist anchors lane B for the rest of the run. Act I ends with 10 cards, 2 Bright, a clear Undertow plan and 14✦ banked.

      Five ways to dress the same game

      Theme workshop

      The mechanics don't depend on a drowned city. Each theme below has to explain the same five things: one currency that buys cards and pays for plays, a deck in a line, paying to reach past the front, four Orders with fixed jobs (fire battery, death swarm, poison-and-pull net, front wall), and three copies merging into one, which thins the deck.

      Keep

      The Drowned City

      Gothic, melancholy, lantern-lit. Darkest Dungeon's dread with a warmer heart.

      Currency
      Embers ✦, the lantern's fuel
      Procession
      A procession of spirits following your light. Reaching is the lantern's beam stretching further into the dark.
      Three-copy merge
      Rekindling: three faint flames braid into one Bright spirit.
      Feels like
      Darkest Dungeon, Inscryption, Hollow Knight
      LampLamp
      HollowHollow
      BrineBrine
      BellBell

      Recommended. Every mechanic already has its fiction: light as currency, reaching into the dark, flames merging. It reads clearly from the key art and is the lowest-risk choice for a demo.

      Best alternative

      Mycelia

      Bioluminescent underworld. Eerie but beautiful, closer to Ori than to horror.

      Currency
      Glowcaps, stored light from the mycelium
      Procession
      A spore-line growing out from your heart-mushroom. Reaching is pushing the network further through the soil.
      Three-copy merge
      Fruiting: three spores fuse into one mature Bloom, and the network gets leaner.
      Feels like
      Hollow Knight's Fungal Wastes, Ori, Spiritfarer
      LampEmbercaps (fire spores)
      HollowRotlings (decomposers)
      BrineThreadweavers (hyphae that pull)
      BellShelfstone (bracket fungus)

      The strongest alternative. Clusters become the mycelial network, so adjacency has a better story than in any other theme, and decay feeding new growth fits the Hollow Order perfectly. Less crowded on Steam than gothic horror. It could also be blended in: a drowned city overgrown with glowing fungus.

      Strong hook

      The Last Midway

      A haunted carnival that only opens at midnight. Eerie and playful at once.

      Currency
      Tickets
      Procession
      Literally a parade, in order. Reaching is bribing the ringmaster to wave an act forward.
      Three-copy merge
      Headliners: three sideshow acts combine into one star act, and the bill gets shorter.
      Feels like
      Inscryption, Cult of the Lamb, Luck be a Landlord
      LampFirebreathers
      HollowClown Car (they keep coming back)
      BrineHook Men (the vaudeville hook pulls)
      BellStrongmen

      The best one-line pitch and the funniest cards. The Procession-as-parade fit is perfect. The risk is tone: whimsy can undercut the strategy feel, and carnival art needs a confident illustrator.

      Widest audience

      The Hive

      Cozy-tactical. Bright, warm and readable. Wingspan's charm on a battlefield.

      Currency
      Honey
      Procession
      The waggle dance: the order your workers leave the hive. Reaching is calling a forager back early.
      Three-copy merge
      Metamorphosis: three larvae pupate into one adult, the most literal merge of all.
      Feels like
      Wingspan, Bugsnax, Hollow Knight
      LampWasps (ranged stings that burn)
      HollowAnts (the colony absorbs losses)
      BrineSpiders (webs pull, venom ticks)
      BellBeetles (armoured shell wall)

      The friendliest and the best fit if iOS matters later. Metamorphosis explains Rekindling better than any other theme. It loses the dread that makes embers feel precious.

      Weakest fit

      Starwreck

      Scrappy salvage sci-fi in a dead fleet's graveyard.

      Currency
      Fuel cells
      Procession
      The launch queue of your drone bay. Reaching is burning fuel to re-sequence the bay.
      Three-copy merge
      Prototyping: three salvaged parts are welded into one prototype.
      Feels like
      FTL, Into the Breach, Cobalt Core
      LampPlasma batteries
      HollowDrone swarms that rebuild
      BrineTractor and acid rigs
      BellBulkhead mechs

      Mechanically fine, but sci-fi tactics is the most crowded shelf on Steam, and fuel cells explain the economy less vividly than light does.

      My pick: keep the Drowned City for the demo, and treat Mycelia as the fallback, or as a visual layer (glowing fungus overgrowing the sunken streets) if the art direction wants more colour.
